Blockchain is a decentralized digital technology used to store and manage data securely. Instead of keeping information in one central server, blockchain stores data in multiple computers called nodes. The information is organized into blocks, and each block is connected to the previous one, forming a chain.

Every block contains transaction data, a timestamp, and a unique cryptographic code called a hash. Once data is added to the blockchain, it becomes extremely difficult to change or delete, making the system transparent and secure.

Blockchain works through a network where participants validate transactions using consensus mechanisms. It removes the need for intermediaries such as banks in many applications.

Although blockchain is commonly associated with cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, its applications go beyond digital currency. It can be used in banking, supply chain management, healthcare, railway systems, smart contracts, digital identity, voting, and record management.

The main advantages of blockchain are security, transparency, decentralization, immutability, and traceability. However, challenges such as scalability, energy consumption, and implementation cost still exist.

Overall, blockchain is an important technology that can make digital systems more secure, transparent, and efficient.
